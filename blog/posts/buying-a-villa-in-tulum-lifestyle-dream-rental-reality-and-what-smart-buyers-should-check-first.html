Owning Property in Mexico: The Fideicomiso Explained Simply

If you're not a Mexican citizen, buying property within 100km of the borders or 50km of the coast (called the "Restricted Zone") means using a fideicomiso, or bank trust. Simply put, a Mexican bank holds the legal title to the property for you. As the beneficiary of this trust, you keep all the ownership rights: you can use the property, sell it, rent it out, or leave it to your heirs. It’s a secure, legal way for non-Mexicans to own coastal property in Mexico.

Real Buyer Risks and Due Diligence

Every property market has its quirks, and Tulum is no different. Smart buyers always do their homework. This means:

  • Legal Checks: Confirming the property title is clean, has no debts, and the seller is legitimate.
  • Zoning & Permits: Verifying the property's zoning allows for your planned use (like short-term rentals) and all construction permits are valid.
  • Environmental Rules: Tulum has clear environmental laws. Knowing these is key to avoiding future problems.
  • Local Expertise: Working with a reputable real estate agent, lawyer, and notary public who understand Mexican property law and the Tulum market.

The Reality of Short-Term Rental Management

Tulum receives steady visitor interest, but owners should still verify demand, seasonality, pricing, and operating costs before relying on rental income. Effective short-term rental management includes:

  • Marketing & Bookings: Listing the property, managing calendars, and attracting guests.
  • Guest Services: Handling check-ins, check-outs, offering local advice, and responding to guest needs.
  • Maintenance & Cleaning: Regular upkeep, repairs, and keeping the property in top condition.
  • Local Rules: Following all local rental laws and tax requirements.
